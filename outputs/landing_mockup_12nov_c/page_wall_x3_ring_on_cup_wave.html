How this works under the hood

What decides whether a hand can do a task, in the order the check takes it. Scroll sideways.

  1. Place the hand

    A search over position and turn finds where to put the task's object and contact points relative to the hand, so that the fingers can best reach the points they serve. For a hand's own hold the place is already known.

  2. Give each contact a finger

    Each finger serves at most one contact point. Every pairing is tried and the one that suits the hand best is kept. A point left without a finger must have its load carried by the others.

  3. Reach and facing

    Each finger is posed to touch its point with the pad or a side of its last or middle segment, and the surface there must face the object. A finger that cannot get there is red, with the reason no_contact.

  4. Friction and direction

    The force the task asks for at the point must lie inside the friction cone of the surface that touches. Red reasons: direction, friction.

  5. Strength

    The joint torques that balance the contact force must be made by the finger's inputs, each within its own range. Red reason: torque.

  6. The verdict for the frame

    Green: reaches, faces, and gives the task's own force. Amber: close, or a point has no finger, and the push on the object can still be made with different forces. Red: the first of these tests that failed. A frame takes its worst finger's verdict.

  7. Headroom

    How many times larger the task's load could get and still be held. Stored only for runs made with --margins.

  8. Not checked yet

    Each frame is checked on its own. Whether the poses join up from one frame to the next, and whether the hand is fast enough and can carry its own inertia, are not checked yet.
