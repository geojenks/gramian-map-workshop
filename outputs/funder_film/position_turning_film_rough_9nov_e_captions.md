Built by `tools/render_force_film.py` from logged data. Rough cut, about 75 s. Times are approximate.

The two halves use different checks. The Hora half checks position and force targets. The human half checks position, and separately whether the touching fingers can turn the ball, because the forces we can derive there show the weight shared out, not the turn.

Simulated and derived magnitudes are comparative, not predictive.

## Limits of the turning check

- The torque to spare is much larger than the torque the turn needs, so its colours are for comparing hands and poses, not for predicting performance.
- Joint end stops carry load without an upper limit in the model the check shares with `follow.py`. This can inflate the torque to spare. For Shadow, a touching finger has a joint at an end stop in 33 of the 36 frames it can hold, from a separate test run of the check, not its results files, so its greens may be generous. We have not yet counted this for the Allegro and LEAP placements made with fingers paired by name.
- A four-fingered hand is not scored on the person's little-finger contact.

## Rebuilding

Inputs are in `inputs_7nov/` and `checks_9nov/` (see its `README.txt`). Commands are in `inputs_position_turning_7nov/render_commands_e.sh`. The film needs no scipy. The turning check does; we installed it for that run only, into a temporary folder on `PYTHONPATH`.
